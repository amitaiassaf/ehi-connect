OffTheRecord — Consumer health data privacy policy

Effective date: [date]. Last updated: [month and year]. Publisher: Ticker Medical Inc., 447 Sutter St, Ste 506-1454, San Francisco, CA 94108. Privacy requests and appeals: contact@offtherecordhealth.com.

1. What this policy covers

OffTheRecord helps you make a copy of medical records on your own Windows computer or Android phone, with identifying details reduced. This policy explains the health information the app handles, why it handles it, where it goes, and your choices and privacy rights.

The app processes records on your device. Its record features do not upload your medical records, identifying profile or results to the publisher. We do not run a server that stores your medical records. Local processing still involves health information; this policy does not treat it as outside privacy law.

The app is paid. Purchases, support and visits to public pages involve separate information described in section 4.

The app is for adults aged 18 and over. Use it for your own records or records you are legally allowed to manage, including a child's records when you have the necessary authority. Your authority must cover access and any sharing you choose. People under 18 may not operate the app.

2. Information handled and where it comes from

The app handles these categories on your device:

  • Identifying details: names, birth date, addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, medical-record and insurance numbers. They come from details you enter and records you import or authorize your hospital to supply. They help the app find and reduce identifying information.
  • Medical records: text, documents, scanned pages and images you add, and information downloaded from your hospital. Depending on your records, this includes conditions, medicines, allergies, tests, clinical dates, visits, procedures, care teams, and physical, mental, reproductive, sexual or genetic health information. Notes can also mention family members and other people.
  • Working information and copies: imported files, text read from scans, results, review decisions, automatic-check flags and dates, and copies prepared for saving or sharing. The app creates these to carry out the features you request.
  • Sign-in information: the hospital supplies an access token and patient context after you authorize a connection. The app uses the token in memory and does not intentionally save it in its files. It does not receive your hospital password. The return-page request in section 4 is separate.

The sources are you, the files you select, the hospital you authorize, and the app's processing of those inputs. The app does not independently obtain records from a hospital you have not chosen and authorized.

3. Why the app uses information and how long it stays

The app uses information to import or download your chosen records, find identifying details, read scans, prepare a copy, show results, record your choices, and save or share selected files. Text recognition runs on your device; pictures are not sent to Google for recognition. The app does not change your hospital chart or give medical advice.

On Windows, workspaces contain redaction details, imported or downloaded records, results and review decisions. They remain until removed or replaced by the app, or you delete the workspace. Workspace > Delete workspace removes its local data; your original files and copies saved elsewhere remain. Workspace > Delete all medical data removes the local data of every workspace and keeps only your agreement to the terms and the app's settings. When you uninstall, you choose whether to keep or delete the app's data, with neither choice preselected; cancelling or an unclear choice keeps it, and deleting also removes your agreement and settings.

On Android, imported records, working data, results and the shareable copy stay in the app's private storage; the app does not save copies to other folders. Later runs can replace or remove working files and old downloads. Remaining private data stays until you use Delete medical data in the app, clear the app's storage or uninstall it. Delete medical data keeps only nonmedical settings and your agreement to the terms. Copies you shared stay with whoever received them.

Windows account controls and Android's app-private storage limit ordinary access. Neither app adds its own encryption. Protect your device and account. The Android app turns off Android's cloud backup and phone-to-phone transfer for its data; some modified or nonstandard devices may not follow these settings. Ordinary deletion is not a promise that a file cannot be recovered from a device or backup.

Removing details does not guarantee anonymity or legal de-identification. Real clinical dates stay in phone copies, and either app can miss details. Read each copy before sharing it.

4. Information outside the app's private storage

Hospital connection and public hosting. Your hospital receives requests needed for sign-in and download. Production hospital sign-in on Windows and Android returns through a GitHub-hosted page at https://offtherecordhealth.com/epic-return/. GitHub receives the page request, including its one-time code, state or error fields and connection information such as an IP address. The code needs a separate secret held by the app before it can be exchanged. The page is not sent your medical records or hospital password. Windows also uses Epic's public hospital directory. GitHub handles hosting information under its privacy statement.

The static public pages do not load advertising or cross-site tracking scripts, set no cookies and use no analytics. GitHub hosts the pages; Cloudflare runs our domain's name service and email forwarding. There is no checkout yet; this section will name any checkout provider before purchases open.

Purchases and support. The app is not for sale yet. When it is, the app store or payment provider that handles your purchase collects payment and account information under its own privacy policy, and we, Ticker Medical Inc., receive the order details needed for sales records, taxes, refunds and licence checks. If you email us, Cloudflare forwards the message and Google stores it in our mailbox; we use it only to answer you and keep it with our business records. Please do not send medical records, identifying profiles, passwords or sign-in codes when contacting us.

5. Who can receive information

Recipients you choose. Saving or sharing can take selected files outside the app's private storage. On Android, Share gives the selected file to the receiving app you choose. A chosen folder, cloud-storage provider, person, organization or AI service can receive the copy and may upload or sync it. The copy can contain health information and remaining identifiers. The recipient's terms and privacy practices govern its handling.

Hospital and hosting providers. Your chosen hospital receives connection and download requests. GitHub receives web requests as described in section 4, rather than a copy of your chart. Local text-recognition components read record content on your device; that does not by itself send content to their suppliers.

Publisher, service providers and affiliates. The record features do not send your chart, identifying profile or results to the publisher or its affiliates. Our email providers, Cloudflare (forwarding) and Google (mailbox), receive health information only if you include it in a message to us, and only to deliver and store that message. GitHub receives web requests for our public pages as described in section 4. No affiliates receive it.

We do not sell your medical records, use them for advertising, or use your records, identifying profile or copies to train a publisher AI model or large language model. Paying for the app is separate from selling health information. Recipients you choose may have different practices; check them before sending a copy.

6. Your choices, requests and appeals

Depending on the law that applies, you can ask whether health information is collected, shared or sold; access it and obtain a copy; learn who received it; request correction or deletion; withdraw consent; and appeal a refusal. You can exercise applicable rights without creating a publisher account. Exercising a right will not result in unlawful discrimination.

Local files. You can view records and results through existing review features and save a copy. On Windows, use Workspace > Delete workspace for one workspace or Workspace > Delete all medical data for all of them, or choose to delete the app's data when you uninstall. On Android, use Delete medical data in the app's ⋮ menu, clear the app's storage in the device's settings (wording varies by device), or uninstall it. Remove separately saved copies from their folders and ask recipients about copies they hold. We cannot remotely inspect, change or delete files on your device. Tell us if these steps do not address your request; this does not limit our duties under applicable law.

You can change details used for redaction and make a new copy. The app does not edit the hospital's source chart; contact your hospital about correcting that record. For correction of information the publisher or a provider actually holds, use the contact below.

Stop future access or processing. You can stop adding or processing records, decline a connection, and revoke the app's hospital authorization in your patient portal. This does not delete existing files or copies already shared. To withdraw consent for publisher-held processing or sharing, email contact@offtherecordhealth.com; we will explain what can stop and any lawful retention that remains.

Submit a request. Email contact@offtherecordhealth.com. Tell us the right you want to exercise, the app platform or version if relevant, the state whose rights you invoke, and a safe way to reply. Do not attach medical records or sign-in secrets. We ask only for information reasonably needed to authenticate and address the request. If we have no copy of the records concerned, we will explain that and help identify local steps rather than ask you to upload them. Responses are free where the law requires.

We respond without undue delay within the applicable legal deadline. Washington and Connecticut requests generally require a response within 45 days of receipt, with one permitted 45-day extension and notice when justified. Nevada generally uses 45 days after authentication, with a permitted extension; an authenticated Nevada deletion request has a 30-day deadline, subject to the law's exceptions. We apply any shorter deadline required and explain any lawful limitation or extension. Connecticut consent-based processing must stop as soon as practicable and within 15 days after revocation where that rule applies.

Appeal. Reply to contact@offtherecordhealth.com with Appeal and identify the decision you disagree with. We explain the result and reasons in writing within 45 days for Washington or Nevada appeals and within 60 days for Connecticut appeals, or sooner where required. If we deny the appeal, we provide the relevant Attorney General's complaint contact or mechanism. [Confirm a working, monitored request and appeal route before this policy takes effect.]
